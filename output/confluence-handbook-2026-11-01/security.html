Project handbook

Reviewed November 1, 2026. Status: current implementation and planned work are distinguished below. Documentation is not certification of production readiness.

Authentication versus authorization

Accounts owns credential/session identity. The marketplace owns business roles and organization membership. The gateway strips untrusted identity headers and forwards signed, short-lived, request-bound assertions with service-specific audiences. Each service must still authorize the requested record and action. Supplying organizationId is not proof of membership.

KYC and legal-party evidence

KYC is a manual evidence/review service. Applicants submit drafts and credentials; independent authorized reviewers record evidence checks and case decisions. Approval requires the documented evidence and a valid expiry. Expired approvals are reported expired; historical decisions remain intact. The service does not itself claim automated AML, sanctions, accreditation, liveness or license-registry checks.

Signatures and binding

Signing uses explicit reviewed snapshots, hashes and verified ceremonies. A guest invitation proves possession of the scoped invitation and signing credential, not independently checked legal identity. A signature alone is not binding. A separate binding workflow exists with role checks, signature/quote readiness, immutable snapshots and a transaction lock. Manual/no-quote paths and financial completeness need an explicit policy.

Network and connectors

Network discoverability is opt-in; company publishing requires membership and an explicit grant. Its private marketplace projection excludes credentials and KYC evidence. Email service requests use service authentication; mailbox tokens/checkpoints remain owned by the API and must not appear in logs or documentation.

Unfinished financial controls

  • Versioned eligibility checks before publication, commitment, binding and payout.
  • Beneficiary/payout-account mandates and independently authorized changes.
  • Clear separation between posting actor, legal contracting party, payer, payee and beneficial owner.
  • Expiry/revocation/provider-outage behavior and auditable exception approvals.
