Purpose

This section connects technical records to business meaning. The current code contains substantial models and immutable evidence, but separate balance fields and event lists do not automatically reconcile into a party-level ledger. Use the child pages to distinguish source facts, decisions, contractual obligations and cash movements.

Four layers of data

LayerExamplesRequired interpretation
Observed factsDocuments, extracted premium/TIV/loss, party/address, currencySource, unit, confidence, period and conflict status.
Decisions and versionsCanonical/workbench revision, model run, pricing version, approvalExact inputs, method/schema version, actor and assumptions.
Contractual obligationsQuote, binding, facility, commitment, premium due, claim/recoveryLegal party roles, effective terms, payer/payee and obligation state.
Financial executionReceipt, funding, collateral transfer, payment, reversalProvider evidence, currency, settlement status and reconciliation.

Data principles

  • Unknown is not zero; committed is not funded; approved is not paid.
  • Use explicit currency and precision. Do not aggregate currencies without a cited FX policy and rate timestamp.
  • Preserve immutable history; corrections/reversals are new referenced records.
  • Separate ownership and authorization from display labels and free-text party names.
  • Use typed relational constraints for core identity/money/references while retaining validated versioned organization extensions.
  • Each field needs an authoritative writer and a documented dependency/rebuild policy.
